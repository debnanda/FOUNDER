# How to Play

Everything you need to go from a $30,000 bank balance to an IPO — the rules, every tab, and a full
sample playthrough. This same guide is also built into the game itself: click **How to Play** on
the title screen.

## The goal

You have $30,000 in savings, one idea, and yourself. Build a product, hire a team, grow revenue,
and reach one of three endings before the money runs out:

| Ending | How |
| --- | --- |
| 🔔 **IPO** | Reach a $100M valuation **and** $400k MRR at the same time, then file from the Money tab. |
| ◆ **Acquisition** | Random offers start arriving once MRR passes $20k. Accept one to cash out instantly at your equity share. |
| ▁ **Bankruptcy** | The one to avoid — cash stays negative for several straight weeks and it's over. |

## Time & controls

The game runs in weekly ticks, roughly 1.6 seconds per week at 1× speed.

| Key | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| `Space` | Pause / resume |
| `1` / `2` / `3` | 1× / 2× / 4× speed |

Progress autosaves to your browser every 4 in-game weeks, so you can close and pick up where you
left off.

## Setup: idea & archetype

**Idea** sets your market size, price per user (ARPU), how fast it spreads by word of mouth,
free-to-paying conversion, infrastructure cost per user, and how many dev points the MVP needs
before you can launch. Bigger markets (social/consumer apps) tend to pay less per user; niche B2B
tools (fintech, dev tools) pay more but grow slower — pick based on the kind of risk you want to
take on.

**Archetype** decides your own starting skill. You're the company's first employee and never draw
a salary:

- **The Hacker** — starts as a skill-8 engineer
- **The Hustler** — starts as a skill-8 marketer
- **The Craftsman** — starts as a skill-8 designer

## Product tab

Engineers generate **dev points** each week (skill × morale × a fixed multiplier, plus a bonus
while crunching). The **focus slider** splits that output between shipping features — which fills
the MVP bar, then raises product appeal — and stability work.

Feature work piles up **tech debt**; stability work pays it down. High debt drives up weekly churn
and can trigger outage events, so don't leave the slider pinned at 100% features forever,
especially after launch.

Once progress reaches the idea's MVP threshold, hit **🚀 Launch v1.0**. Launch seeds you with a
few hundred curious early users and switches on the growth engine.

**Crunch mode** gives a solid output boost but drains morale fast — people quit once morale
bottoms out. Use it in short bursts before a launch or a raise, not as a steady state.

## Team tab

Four roles:

- **Engineers** — build the product
- **Designers** — raise appeal and cut churn
- **Marketers** — stretch every ad dollar further (lower CAC)
- **Sales** — convert more free users into paying ones

Hire from a rotating pool of candidates that refreshes automatically every 6 weeks, or pay a small
fee for a fresh batch anytime. Firing costs one month's severance and dents everyone else's
morale.

Morale drifts toward a baseline, pulled up by office perks and down by crunch or negative cash.
Anyone whose morale gets low enough risks burning out and quitting on their own. **Office perks**
(four levels, one-time purchases) raise the morale ceiling for the whole team at a permanently
higher weekly rent.

## Growth tab

Set a weekly **marketing budget**; it buys new users at your current **CAC**, which climbs as you
saturate more of the total market — hiring marketers pushes it back down.

**Organic growth** comes from word of mouth: existing users × the idea's virality × product
appeal. **Churn** is driven mostly by tech debt (designers reduce it). The funnel card shows
paying customers, ARPU, and churn rate directly.

## Money tab

Four funding rounds, each sold for newly-issued equity that dilutes you and every existing
investor:

| Round | Equity sold | Unlocks at |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pre-seed | 10% | Always open, priced at a flat $1.5M |
| Seed | 15% | Launched + 2,000 users |
| Series A | 18% | $60k+ MRR |
| Series B | 15% | $300k+ MRR |

There's a cooldown between raises, so time them deliberately. Later rounds price off your live
valuation, so growth before raising pays off directly.

## Random events

From launch onward there's a real chance each week of an event modal: press features, viral
moments, outages, a funded competitor, poach attempts on your best people, enterprise contract
offers, and acquisition offers. Each gives you a genuine trade-off — do the press interview and
risk a live crash, or pass quietly; fight an outage with an all-hands (morale hit, debt paid down)
or patch it and eat the churn. There's no universally right answer — weigh it against your current
cash and product stability.

## Walkthrough — one full run

**Week 0 — Found.** Name the company, pick an archetype and an idea. You start solo with $30k and
a default focus split leaning toward features.

**Weeks 1–8 — Build the MVP.** Hire one or two more engineers as cash allows. Keep an eye on tech
debt — nudge focus toward stability if it creeps up, so launch doesn't start with a churn problem.
Once progress crosses the MVP threshold, the Product tab lights up **🚀 Launch v1.0**.

**Week ~8 — Launch.** Click launch. A few hundred early users show up immediately, and the growth
engine turns on: marketing spend and organic word of mouth start moving the user count.

**Weeks 9–20 — Find the growth loop.** On Growth, start with a modest marketing budget — CAC is
cheapest before you've saturated the market. Hire a designer to lift appeal and cut churn; take a
Pre-seed raise early if cash is tight. Handle whatever events show up — accept press once
stability is comfortably high, amplify a viral moment with paid ads if you can afford it.

**Weeks 20–50 — Raise and scale.** Cross the user threshold to unlock Seed; push MRR up to unlock
Series A. Put fresh capital into more engineers, a second designer, and marketers or sales to
compound growth and conversion. Keep focus balanced — staying in pure feature-mode after launch
will bury you in churn.

**Weeks 50–100+ — Push for the exit.** Keep MRR and valuation climbing toward Series B. Watch for
acquisition offers once MRR passes $20k — a strong early offer can be a legitimate win if the
payout is good. Otherwise keep compounding until valuation and MRR both clear the IPO bar, then
file from the Money tab.

**Staying alive.** If a bad stretch pushes cash negative, you get a short grace window before
bankruptcy. Cut the marketing budget, pause hiring, and lean on any open funding round to get back
to positive net before the clock runs out.

## Tips

- Don't hire faster than your revenue can support — salaries are a fixed weekly cost regardless of
  how growth is going.
- Watch the runway indicator in the top bar; it's the earliest warning you'll get that spending
  needs to come down.
- Designers are the main lever against churn — useful even before you'd think to prioritize one.
- A raise's price is set by your metrics at that moment, so a burst of growth right before raising
  is worth more than the same growth spread out.
